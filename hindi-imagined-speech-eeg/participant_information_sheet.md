## 1. Why are you being invited?

You are being invited to take part in a research study at Mahindra University. We are developing a research dataset of EEG (electroencephalography) signals recorded while Hindi-speaking adults silently imagine saying Hindi words.

The study is intended to support research on brain-computer interfaces (BCIs), especially future Hindi-language BCI research. It is a research study and is **not a medical test, diagnosis, treatment, or assessment of your mental health**.

## 2. Why is this study being done?

Most imagined-speech BCI research and publicly available datasets have focused on languages other than Hindi. This study aims to create a structured Hindi imagined-speech EEG resource that can be used to study whether EEG signals contain information associated with different imagined Hindi words.

The research team may also conduct proof-of-concept computer analysis to distinguish imagined-word conditions from rest.

## 3. Who can take part?

The current protocol proposes healthy adult Hindi-speaking volunteers aged **18–35 years**. You will be asked screening questions about language ability, vision, and relevant health history before recording.

The research team will determine eligibility using the study's approved screening procedure.

## 4. What will happen if I participate?

### Before recording

- The researcher will explain the study and answer your questions.
- You will complete the consent process before recording begins.
- You will complete a short participant screening procedure.
- The EEG equipment will be fitted and the signal quality will be checked.
- If an eye tracker is retained in the final setup, it may be calibrated for gaze verification and artifact review.

### During the task

You will sit comfortably in front of a screen. Hindi words will be presented one at a time. Your task is to **silently imagine saying the displayed Hindi word in your own voice**.

You should:

- look at the screen as instructed;
- silently imagine saying the word;
- remain as still as reasonably possible; and
- avoid speaking, whispering, lip-syncing, or intentionally moving your lips or tongue during the imagined-speech period.

### Trial timing

Each trial follows this sequence:

| Stage | Duration | What you do |
|---|---:|---|
| Fixation | 500 ms | Look at the centre marker and remain still |
| Word display | 300 ms | Read the Hindi word on the screen |
| Imagined speech | 3,000 ms | Silently imagine saying the word |
| Rest/reset | 1,000 ms | Relax before the next trial |

There are **6 randomized Hindi word classes/stimuli** in the current protocol.

### Recording blocks

The current protocol specifies:

- Practice: **55 trials (~4 minutes)**
- Main Block 1: **110 trials (~8.8 minutes)**
- Main Block 2: **110 trials (~8.8 minutes)**
- Main Block 3: **110 trials (~8.8 minutes)**
- Main Block 4: **110 trials (~8.8 minutes)**

Scheduled rest breaks are provided between blocks. The exact break/session arrangement will follow the final approved implementation.

## 5. What is EEG?

EEG is a non-invasive method for recording electrical activity associated with brain activity using sensors placed on the scalp. The equipment records signals from your scalp; it does **not** send electrical stimulation into your brain.

The final EEG device is still being confirmed by the study team. The participant-facing materials will be updated if the approved device or its relevant operating procedure changes.

## 6. Are there any risks or discomforts?

This is considered a minimal-risk, non-invasive study under the current protocol.

Possible temporary discomforts include:

- mild pressure from the EEG headset;
- temporary scalp dryness or stickiness associated with the recording setup;
- mild tiredness or mental fatigue from concentrating for an extended session; and
- possible mild discomfort from remaining still or wearing the equipment.

The headset can be adjusted, breaks are provided, and the equipment can be removed immediately if you are uncomfortable. You may pause or stop the session at any time without penalty.

No electrical stimulation is applied.

## 7. Are there benefits to me?

You may not receive a direct personal benefit from participating. The main potential benefit is to research and future development of Hindi-language brain-computer interface technology.

The study does not provide a diagnosis, treatment, or individual assessment of your brain or mental health.

## 8. Is participation voluntary?

Yes. Taking part is completely voluntary.

You may:

- ask questions before, during, or after the session;
- request a pause;
- stop participating at any time; and
- withdraw without penalty or effect on your academic standing or relationship with Mahindra University.

## 9. What happens to my data?

You will be assigned a study ID. Your name and other direct identifying information will be kept separately from your EEG research data.

Research data will be stored on secure institutional systems with access limited to authorized members of the research team.

The research team may publish scientific findings from the study.

## 10. Future use and data sharing

With appropriate consent and in accordance with institutional policy, **anonymized EEG data** may be used in future brain-computer interface classification research and may be shared as an open research dataset.

Only data suitable for the approved sharing arrangement will be released. Direct personal identifiers will not be included in the public research dataset.

If data have been irreversibly anonymized and publicly released, it may no longer be possible to identify or remove an individual's data from copies already released.

## 11. How long will my data be retained?

The current participant materials specify a **minimum retention period of five years**, subject to institutional policy and the approved study requirements.

## 12. What if I want to withdraw?

You can tell a member of the research team that you want to stop participating.

Where your data are still identifiable or reversibly coded, you may request deletion in accordance with the study's data-management procedure.

Once data have been irreversibly anonymized and publicly released, individual withdrawal from those released copies may no longer be possible.
